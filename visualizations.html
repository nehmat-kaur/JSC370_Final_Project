Interactive Visualizations

This page presents interactive visualizations that reinforce key findings from the modeling section. The visualizations focus on variables such as genre, tempo, speechiness, acousticness, and energy, which were found to significantly influence Spotify track popularity.

1. Tempo vs Popularity (Interactive Scatterplot)

Tempo was a moderately important predictor in both the linear regression and random forest models.

Interpretation: This scatterplot shows a weak positive trend between tempo and popularity, but with high variability. While the trend line slopes slightly upward, there’s no strong linear association, indicating that tempo alone is not a strong determinant of popularity but may still contribute modestly.

2. Speechiness vs Popularity (Interactive Scatterplot)

Speechiness had a notable negative impact on predicted popularity in both the linear regression and regression tree models.

Interpretation: The plot shows a clear downward trend: tracks with high speechiness values (e.g., ≥ 0.8) generally have lower popularity. This aligns with modeling results, where speechiness had one of the strongest negative coefficients in the linear regression.

3. Popularity by Genre (Interactive Boxplot)

Genre was the most dominant predictor across all models and strongly influenced popularity.

Interpretation: This boxplot confirms that genre plays a crucial role in a track’s popularity. Genres such as Alternative, Hip-Hop, and Electronic tend to show higher popularity, while Children’s Music, Opera, and Classical are associated with lower popularity — matching results from both linear regression and the regression tree.

4. Popularity by Acousticness Level (Boxplot)

Acousticness was the first splitting variable in the regression tree, suggesting a fundamental split in track type.

Interpretation: Tracks with low acousticness tend to be more popular, as shown by the higher median and wider distribution of popularity scores. This confirms the first split in the regression tree, where acousticness separated the data early on.

5. Interactive Spearman Correlation Heatmap

This heatmap provides a holistic view of monotonic relationships between numeric audio features and popularity.

Interpretation: The heatmap confirms that most individual features have only weak monotonic correlations with popularity. This underscores the need for multivariate models to detect interactions and nonlinear effects.

Summary

The visualizations reinforce key findings from the modeling analysis. Genre stood out as the most influential feature, while speechiness, tempo, and acousticness showed moderate associations with popularity. These plots not only validate model insights but also offer intuitive ways to explore how different features affect listener preferences on Spotify.
